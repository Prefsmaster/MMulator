## 13.25.1 Introduction (p. 126)

With the 80-character board, to be built into the P2000T, 80 characters can be displayed on one
line. Two simple BASIC instructions switch from 40 to 80 characters and back. In this way one
program can make use of both formats. **In the 80-character setting the horizontal scroll is
switched off. Scrolling has no meaning in that case.**

> *"In de 80-teken stand is de horizontale scroll uitgeschakeld. Scrollen heeft in dat geval
> geen zin."*

The board contains a number of special components which must meet stricter requirements than
those given in the standard specification. It is therefore inadvisable to assemble the board
yourself.

Installing the board is a job that can only be carried out by experienced electronics
hobbyists. Among other things, **a 24-pin IC must be desoldered from the CPU board.** Special
tooling is required for this.

## 13.25.2 Circuit description (p. 126)

*(see the schematic under 13.25.3)*

The characters that appear on the screen are generated by a character-generator IC (**SAA5050**)
under the control of a clock-generator IC (**SAA5020**). Both ICs are identical to those used in
colour-TV sets with viewdata/teletext. **They run at a clock frequency of 6 MHz and give 40
characters on a line.**

**Trials have shown that the character generator, far outside its specifications, still works at
double speed.** Sometimes one sees, at the position of a switch-over character, a small block or
a few dashes instead of a space, but it is surprising how much else goes well: text, colour,
graphics, double height and blinking. **A precondition for all this is a very precisely chosen
mutual alignment of the clock pulses.**

> *"Uit proeven is gebleken dat de karaktergenerator, ver buiten zijn specificaties, met dubbele
> snelheid ook nog werkt. Soms ziet men dan op de plaats van een omschakelteken een blokje of
> wat streepjes in plaats van een spatie, doch het is verrassend te zien hoeveel er verder goed
> gaat: tekst, kleur, grafisch, dubbele hoofte en knipperen. Voorwaarde bij dit alles is een
> zeer precies gekozen onderlinge ligging van de klokimpulsen."*

**The starting point is a crystal oscillator of 24 MHz, followed by a divide-by-two which
ensures a very accurate duty cycle of 50%. From this, some other pulse shapes are derived which
normally come out of the IC SAA5020 twice as slowly. A switching IC (LS157) switches over from
the outputs of the SAA5020 to the newly generated fast pulses. In this way the switchable line
length is achieved.**

> *"Uitgangspunt is een kristaloscillator van 24 MHz, gevolgd door een tweedeler die zorg draagt
> voor een zeer nauwkeurige dutycycle van 50%. Hiervan worden enkele andere impulsvormen
> afgeleid die normaal tweemaal zo langzaam uit het IC SAA5020 komen. Een schakel-IC (LS157)
> schakelt om van de uitgangen van de SAA5020 naar de nieuw opgewekte snelle impulsen. Op deze
> wijze vindt de omschakelbare regellengte plaats."*

**The control of the switch-over is done by a circuit that reacts to an output instruction. This
is the half S74. This also switches off the horizontal scroll via pin 1 of IC34. The "state" of
the switch has been made readable by means of the circuit above the half LS74.**

> *"De besturing van de omschakelaar geschiedt door een circuit dat op een output-instructie
> reageert. Dit is de halve S74. Deze schakelt tevens via pen 1 van IC34 de horizontale scroll
> uit. De "stand" van de schakelaar is uitleesbaar gemaakt met behulp van de schakeling boven de
> halve LS74."*

> **Translator's note — the two claims in that last paragraph are the whole port model.** The
> circuit that decodes the `OUT` instruction lives *on the add-on board* (the half 74S74), and
> the read-back path is *also* on the board (the buffer/gate group drawn above the half 74LS74).
> Neither is on the motherboard. §13.25.9's presence-probe recipe confirms this behaviourally.

## 13.25.3 Schematic of the 80-character board (p. 127)

*Figure — not reproduced here. Transcribed contents:*

**Clock chain.** A 24 MHz crystal with an 8T98 buffer, 18 pF capacitors, a 10 kΩ resistor, a
BC546 transistor and a 220 Ω resistor forms the oscillator. Its output feeds an **S74** wired as
a divide-by-two, giving **12 MHz**. The 12 MHz drives an **LS92** divider (pins 5, 6, 14 in; 8,
10, 7 out), which in turn clocks a chain of three **LS74** flip-flop stages and an **LS02**.

**Signals tapped from the SAA5020** (labelled boxes feeding the LS74 chain and the LS157 inputs):

| Label on schematic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `5020 F6in Pen2` | SAA5020 pin 2 — 6 MHz clock input |
| `5020 F6 out Pen3` | SAA5020 pin 3 — 6 MHz clock output |
| `5020 F1 Pen4` | SAA5020 pin 4 — 1 MHz character-rate clock |
| `5020 LOSE Pen13` | SAA5020 pin 13 — LOSE (load/output shift enable) |
| `5020 RACK Pen24` | SAA5020 pin 24 — RACK (row address clock) |

**The LS157 multiplexer outputs** — i.e. what the rest of the machine receives, either the
SAA5020's real signal or the board's doubled-rate substitute:

| LS157 output goes to | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `5050 F6 Pen19` | SAA5050 pin 19 — 6/12 MHz dot clock |
| `5050 F1 Pen20` | SAA5050 pin 20 — 1/2 MHz character clock |
| `5050 LOSE Pen26` | SAA5050 pin 26 — LOSE |
| `IC36 RACK Pen5` | IC36 pin 5 — row address clock into the motherboard's address logic |

**Motherboard signals wired into the board's control logic** (via LS02 gates and 8T98 buffers):
`IC54 pen7`, `IC33 pen11`, `IC34 pen3` (feeding the read-back path); `IC54 pen15`, `IC38 pen5`,
`IC34 pen1`, `IC47 pen13` (feeding the S74 mode latch). A further input labelled **`80 kar`**
enters through a 1 kΩ resistor.

> **Translator's note.** The board's position-number convention is `ICnn` ↔ `71nn` on the old
> CPU board — confirmed by cross-referencing this schematic's labels against the wire list in
> §13.25.7 (`IC34 pen3` ↔ `7134 pen3`, `IC33 pen11` ↔ `7133 pen11`, `IC54 pen7/15` ↔ `7154
> pen7/15`, `IC47 pen13` ↔ `7147 pen13`, `IC38 pen5` ↔ `7138 pen5`). So **IC34 = position 7134 =
> a 74LS273**, whose pin 1 is the asynchronous master-reset — this is the horizontal-scroll
> register the board clears in 80-column mode.
>
> The `80 kar` input with its 1 kΩ pull-up is the hardware forcing contact described in
> §13.25.9.

## 13.25.4 Mounting location of the 80-character board (p. 127)

Because quite a lot of wiring has to be changed, an installation method was chosen in which
**the SAA5020 is desoldered from the board** (many of the connections used are located on this
IC). **Above the position of the removed IC an auxiliary board is mounted, on which the
last-named IC is fitted again.** A few connections must then still be made between the new board
and a number of places on the CPU board.

> *"...is gekozen voor een inbouwwijze, waarbij de SAA5020 uit de print wordt losgenomen (op dit
> IC bevinden zich veel van de gebruikte aansluitingen). Boven de plaats van het verwijderde IC
> wordt een hulpprintje gemonteerd, waarop laatstgenoemd IC weer wordt aangebracht."*

> **Translator's note — this is the point most often misremembered.** The IC that is removed and
> re-seated on the daughterboard is the **SAA5020**, the clock/timing generator — *not* the
> SAA5050 character generator. The SAA5050 stays soldered in place on the motherboard. §13.25.7
> names the part explicitly ("the IC with type number SAA5020 … an IC with 24 pins"), and
> §13.25.7's assembly step says to insert the board "into the socket where the SAA5020
> previously sat." The SAA5050 is a 28-pin device; the article's "a 24-pin IC must be
> desoldered" (§13.25.1) matches the SAA5020's DIL-24 package.

## 13.25.7 Guidelines for installing the 80-character board (p. 129–132)

### Introduction (p. 129)

There are **two versions of the CPU board** which differ considerably in construction. The
conversion is described for both versions. We urgently advise you to read the whole description
carefully and only then decide whether you will carry out the conversion yourself. Quite some
experience is required to do all this without much trouble. And if you do it, follow the
instructions accurately and keep checking every operation you have carried out.

### Unpacking and desoldering the SAA5020 (p. 129)

- Remove the seven cross-head screws in the base plate. Turn the unit over and take the top side
  off the case.
- Remove the "slot box" from the CPU board by undoing the cross-head screws in the slots.
- Remove the memory-expansion board that sits behind the slot box, if present.
- Pull loose the small connectors of the power supply, the cassette recorder, the keyboard and
  the RESET button.
- Undo the four cross-head screws holding the large board in the case, and take the board out of
  the case.
  If your P2000T has a new board, a tin-plate modulator box is fitted against the aluminium rear
  side of the P2000T. Pull the connector and the small black coaxial cable off the modulator so
  the board can be released. On the first version of the CPU board the modulator box is located
  at the left rear of the CPU board. It can be pulled off, since it is connected to the board via
  a connector.
- Find on the large board the IC with type number **SAA5020**. It is an IC with **24 pins**. On
  the old board the position number **7112** is printed at this IC. On the new board we find
  position number **7284**.
  On a P2000T from the first series the IC is fitted in a socket. That saves a lot of work. In
  later versions the IC is soldered in place.
  Take the SAA5020 out of the board. Desoldering works well with a special attachment for the
  soldering iron, with which all the pins of the IC can be heated at once. With tweezers the IC
  can then be lifted out of the board. Ask around whether anyone has such a thing for 24-pin
  ICs. Someone handy with a desoldering pump or desoldering braid can do it this way.

> **Translator's note — an internal inconsistency in the source.** The text above gives the new
> board's position number as **7284**, but the connection diagram in §13.25.11 labels the
> SAA5020 as **7204** (and the SAA5050 beside it as 7203). The 72xx numbering visible on that
> diagram runs from 7201 to about 7255, with no 7284 anywhere. **7204 is almost certainly
> correct** and the running text a typo. Both readings verified against the scan at 400 dpi.

*(1st continuation, p. 130)*

- Clear the resulting holes of solder residue and solder a good IC socket into the board at this
  position.

**Warning!** Treat the SAA5020 very carefully. It is sensitive to static electricity. Place it in
the socket on the 80-character board as quickly as possible. Not much can happen to the IC then.

### Connections on the CPU board (p. 130)

When the new version of the CPU board was designed, a conversion to 80 characters was already
taken into account. The further course of the conversion is therefore rather different for the
old and the new version of the P2000T.

### Connection on the OLD type of CPU board (p. 130)

- Find the IC of type `..LS74`, position number **7104**, and cut through with a sharp knife the
  track on the top side of the board that leads to **pin 6** of this IC (see drawing below).
- Find the IC of type `..LS273`, position number **7134**, and cut through the track on the top
  side of the board that leads to **pin 1** of this IC (see drawing).

*Figure — two detail drawings showing the tracks to be cut ("Onderbreken" = interrupt/cut),
between 7104 (74LS74) / 7103 (74LS02) and between 7134 (74LS273) / 7133 (8304).*

*(2nd continuation, p. 131)*

- Study the diagram of the cursor correction below.

*Figure — `IC 04 pen 6` → 330 Ω → node; node → 680 Ω → ground and → 1 nF → ground; node → `IC 01
pen 10` and `IC 01 pen 7`.*

- Solder a resistor of 680 Ω and a capacitor of 1 nF, parallel to each other, between pin 7 and
  pin 10 of IC7101, and a resistor of 330 Ω between pin 6 of IC7104 and pin 10 of IC7101. Mount
  the components as close as possible above the ICs, or place them on the underside of the
  board.
- **Insert the 80-character board into the socket in which the SAA5020 previously sat**, in such
  a way that the metal modulator box can be put back afterwards. To gain some room, the blue
  capacitors on the large board may be pressed somewhat askew. The auxiliary board must be
  placed as close as possible against the large board.
- Plug the small connector with the loose wires onto the auxiliary board and lead the wires to
  the correct ICs (see the drawing below and the connection diagram under 13.25.10). Solder them
  firmly against the side of the IC sockets. The wires may be shortened to the most convenient
  length.

*Figure — 9-way connector, wire side. Top row: `7134 pen3`, `7134 pen1`, `7133 pen11`, `7154
pen15` (plus a keyed/blanked position). Bottom row: `7154 pen7`, `7147 pen13`, `7138 pen5`.*

### Connection on the NEW type of CPU board (p. 132)

- Study the diagram of the cursor correction below.

*Figure — `J7 pnt2` → 330 Ω → node; node → 680 Ω and 1 nF → ground; node → `J7 pnt1`.*

- With a sharp knife, break the connection between pads 1 and 2 marked **J8**. This is in the
  vicinity of the row of pins for the RAM board.
- Break the connection between pads 1 and 2 marked **J7**. This is in the vicinity of the printer
  connector.
- Solder a resistor of 680 Ω and a capacitor of 1 nF, parallel to each other, between pad 1 of J7
  and the ground plane to which the printer connector is soldered. It may be necessary to scrape
  the green lacquer away at that spot.
- Press the auxiliary board, on which the SAA5020 has already been fitted, into the mounted
  socket. The small connector on the auxiliary board must be as far as possible removed from the
  printer connector. Note that the notch in the SAA8020 then points in the same direction as that
  of all the other ICs on the large board.
- Plug the small connector with the loose wires onto the auxiliary board and lead the wires to
  the correct ICs (see the drawing below and the connection diagram under 13.25.11) and solder
  them firmly against the side of the IC sockets. The wires may be shortened to the most
  convenient length.

*Figure — 9-way connector, wire side. Top row: `7222 pen13`, `7222 pen1`, `7221 pen1`, `7251
pen15`. Bottom row: `7245 pen3`, `7245 pen13`, `7247 pen1`.*

> **Translator's note.** "SAA8020" in the penultimate bullet is a typo in the original for
> SAA5020. Note also that the new board's equivalent of the old board's scroll-register clear is
> `7222 pen1` — 7222 is a **74LS273** on the §13.25.11 diagram, matching the old board's 7134
> exactly.

## 13.25.8 Final assembly and checking of the 80-character board (p. 133)

- Put the large board back in the case of the P2000T.
- Reconnect the power connector, the cassette recorder, the keyboard and the RESET plug.
- Refit the metal modulator box, or reconnect it.
- Reconnect the earth lead to the aluminium rear side of the P2000T.
- Fit the BASIC-NL interpreter and connect the P2000T to the monitor or the TV set.
- Switch the equipment on. **The normal text must appear on the screen.**

- Enter: **`OUT 0,1`**
- **The picture must now become a factor of two narrower, where a small block or dash before
  PHILIPS CASSETTE BASIC is normal.**
- Enter: **`OUT 0,0`**
- **The picture again has its original width.**
- If something does not work properly, check every operation carried out once more, step by step.
- Fit the slots and the cover.

> *"Het beeld moet nu een factor twee smaller worden, waarbij een blokje of streepje voor
> PHILIPS CASSETTE BASIC normaal is."*

## 13.25.9 Use (p. 133–134)

The 80-character board offers the user of a P2000T the possibility of displaying either 80 or 40
characters per line on the screen. **At 80 characters per line the characters are twice as narrow
as normal.** For good legibility the use of a good monochrome monitor is therefore recommended. A
normal TV receiver (with only an aerial connection) offers too little resolving power. A colour
or black-and-white television set with an RGB or SCART connection can do it reasonably, provided
the picture size is not too small. Otherwise the letters "drown" in the pattern of colour
stripes. A special colour monitor with extra-fine stripes does not have these problems.

**On RESET the 40-character setting is chosen automatically.**

**The switch-over is done in software:**

```
OUT 0,1   gives 80 characters
OUT 0,0   gives 40 characters
```

**The "state" can be read out with:**

```
A = INP(&H70)
```

**In which:**

```
A = 0  at 40 characters
A = 1  at 80 characters
```

**By switching back and forth a few times between 80 and 40 characters and each time checking
whether this has been taken over, a program can "see" whether an 80-character board is
present.**

> *"Bij RESET wordt automatisch de 40 karakter-stand gekozen."*
>
> *"Door een paar maal heen en weer te schakelen tussen 80 en 40 tekens en steeds te kijken of
> dit is overgenomen, kan een programma "zien" of een 80-karakterprint aanwezig is."*

*(continued, p. 134)*

At the time the 80-character board was developed, the programs were not yet adapted in the sense
that they took care of the switch-over. **Provision has therefore been made for a contact with
which the P2000T switches over to 80 characters per line, namely by briefly connecting this
contact to ground. It is however unwise to make use of this. The programs for which this is
meaningful switch the P2000T over to 80 characters themselves. Other programs often make use of
the left-hand and right-hand screen of 40 characters each. In the 80-character setting the data
of both screen halves would disturb each other.**

> *"Andere programma's maken vaak gebruik van het linker en rechter scherm van elk 40 tekens. In
> de 80-karakterstand zouden de gegevens van beide beeldscherm-helften elkaar storen."*

## What this settles (translator's summary — not the article's words)

Mapped onto the six open questions recorded in `docs/P2000T-reference.md` §5 before the scan
arrived. **All six are answered**, though two only by inference rather than explicit statement.

| # | Question | Answer | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What doubles the fetch cadence? | **Reading (a), refined.** A 24 MHz crystal ÷2 → 12 MHz on the board generates doubled-rate copies of the SAA5020's character-timing outputs (F6, F1, LOSE, RACK); an **LS157 multiplexer** selects board-generated or SAA5020-native. The SAA5020 itself is **not** overclocked — it keeps running at 6 MHz and keeps generating sync and field timing; only its character-rate outputs are bypassed. | §13.25.2, §13.25.3 — explicit |
| 2 | Where is the port-`0x00` latch? | **On the board.** The half 74S74 reacts to the output instruction; the read-back circuit sits above the half 74LS74. Confirmed behaviourally by §13.25.9's presence-probe: without the board, a write is *not* "taken over" on read-back. | §13.25.2, §13.25.9 — explicit |
| 3 | Character cell geometry | Characters are **twice as narrow**; the picture keeps its overall width. Since the SAA5020 still sets line and field timing, the 64 µs line and the active window are unchanged — the same active window simply carries 80 half-width cells. | §13.25.9 explicit on "twice as narrow"; unchanged line timing is **inferred** from the SAA5020 remaining in circuit |
| 4 | Glyph source | **Unchanged — the SAA5050 stays in place** and is simply clocked at 12 MHz. Colour, graphics, double height and blinking all still work. So the existing font table, the inverted-colour trick and the national remaps all continue to apply. | §13.25.2, §13.25.4 — explicit |
| 5 | Interaction with the pan register | **The horizontal scroll is switched off in hardware** — the mode latch drives pin 1 (asynchronous master reset) of the 74LS273 scroll register, clearing it. Not "ignored", not "preserved": *cleared*. | §13.25.1, §13.25.2 explicit; that pin 1 of a 74LS273 is the async clear is a **datasheet inference** |
| 6 | Contention consequences | Follows from #1 and #3: the character-fetch rate goes 1 MHz → 2 MHz, so **40 → 80 fetch slots per active line, each half as long**, within an unchanged active window. Total fetch-eligible time per line is unchanged. | **Inferred**, not stated — the article never discusses CPU/video bus contention |

### Additional facts the article supplies that were not on the question list

- **Reset default is 40 columns**, stated outright (§13.25.9) — previously carried in the
  reference doc without a citation, now sourced.
- **The VRAM row really is two 40-character screens side by side.** §13.25.9's warning that
  programs using "the left-hand and right-hand screen of 40 characters each" would see "the data
  of both screen halves disturb each other" in 80-column mode only makes sense if the two
  screens are horizontally adjacent within one row — i.e. **an 80-byte row stride**, with the
  pan register sliding a 40-wide window across it. This independently confirms the reference
  doc's flag that §4's `charRow * 40` fetch-address pseudocode uses the wrong stride.
- **A documented rendering artifact**: at 12 MHz the SAA5050 is far outside spec, and a
  switch-over (control) character that should render as a space "sometimes" renders as a small
  block or a few dashes. The article treats this as expected — the commissioning procedure
  (§13.25.8) calls the block before "PHILIPS CASSETTE BASIC" *normal*. The article gives no root
  cause and no rule for when it happens.
- **A software presence-probe protocol**: write, read back, repeat. This requires that on a
  machine *without* the board, port `0x70` bit 0 does **not** track writes to port `0x00`.
- **A hardware forcing contact** (the `80 kar` input, pulled up by 1 kΩ) that switches to 80
  columns when momentarily grounded, provided for software of the era that predates the mode
  switch. The article explicitly advises against using it.
- **Two CPU board revisions exist** with materially different layouts, and the newer one was
  designed with this conversion already in mind (J7/J8 jumper links provided for it).
- **A cursor-correction RC network** (330 Ω / 680 Ω / 1 nF) is added during installation on both
  revisions — an analogue timing tweak with no obvious digital-emulation consequence, recorded
  here only for completeness.

### What the article does NOT answer

- **The exact topology by which the doubled RACK/F1 reaches the video address counters.** The
  board wires into five motherboard ICs (IC33, IC34, IC38, IC47, IC54 on the old revision) whose
  types are not all given, and the P2000T CPU schematic would be needed to trace it properly.
  For emulation this does not matter — the *observable* result (80 distinct characters fetched
  and displayed per line) is stated plainly in §13.25.9 — but it does mean the fetch-slot
  *sub-structure* within each 0.5 µs slot is no better known than it is for 40-column mode, and
  §4a's "one parameter that is NOT derivable" remains underivable here too.
- **The root cause and exact rule of the control-character artifact.** "Sometimes", with no
  further characterisation.
- **Any bus-contention discussion whatsoever.** The article is a construction guide; the
  CPU/video collision behaviour this project models is simply not its subject.
